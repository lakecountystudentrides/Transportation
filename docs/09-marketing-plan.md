# Marketing Plan — Lake County, FL

## Ground rule
**Do not solicit inside schools or licensed daycares** without explicit permission from administration — most Florida public schools and licensed child-care centers have policies against vendors distributing flyers to students/families or approaching parents on campus without prior approval, and violating that can burn the relationship you'll eventually want (a school/daycare that trusts you can become a referral source or even a contract client, but only if you approach it correctly). Always go through the front office and ask for their vendor/community-partner policy before distributing anything.

## Channels

**Google Business Profile (do this day one, it's free and drives local search — "ranks locally"):**
Create and fully complete a Google Business Profile under the transportation-services category, with your real service area, hours, phone, and photos of the vehicle/car seats (builds trust visually). Ask every satisfied first customer to leave a Google review — this is the single highest-leverage free thing you can do for local search ranking, more than anything on the website itself.

**Local Facebook groups:**
Join and genuinely participate (don't just drop ads) in Lake County parent/community groups — city-specific "[City] Moms/Parents" groups, "Lake County Buy Sell Trade," school-specific parent groups where posting is allowed. Introduce yourself as a local parent-run business, be transparent about pricing and safety credentials, and answer questions honestly. Word travels fast in these groups both ways — treat every interaction as a permanent public reference.

**Facebook/Instagram business pages + local paid ads:**
Post regularly: safety-credential highlights, a "meet your driver" post, customer testimonials (with permission), before/after-school-run photos (with parent permission for any child imagery — get explicit written media consent, separate from the transportation agreement). Run small geo-targeted ads ($5–$15/day) to parents in your service-area zip codes once you have testimonials/reviews to show — don't run paid ads before you have social proof.

**Daycares, schools, sports organizations, tutoring centers, churches, community organizations:**
Approach as a B2B partnership conversation, not a solicitation: "I run a background-checked transportation service for families — would you be open to me leaving business cards at your front desk / being listed as a recommended resource for families who ask about transportation?" Many daycares field this question from parents constantly and are glad to have a vetted answer. Bring your insurance certificate and background-check documentation to this conversation — it's the credential that gets you past "we can't recommend just anyone."

**Referral program:**
Give existing customers a concrete incentive to refer — e.g., $25 account credit for both the referrer and the new customer after the new customer's first completed week of recurring service (structure it so it rewards real recurring customers, not one-off bookings, to avoid gaming).

**Flyers:**
Community bulletin boards (libraries, community centers, laundromats, local coffee shops), sports league fields/dugouts (ask the league, not individual parents, for permission), church bulletin boards (ask the office).

## First 25 customers
1. Personally message every parent you already know in your target area — friends, neighbors, former coworkers, church/community contacts — with a direct, non-spammy explanation of the business and a launch offer (e.g., 20% off first month).
2. Post in 3–5 local Facebook parent groups with a genuine introduction post (not an ad) — include your background-check/insurance credentials up front.
3. Set up and fully complete Google Business Profile; ask your first 3–5 customers for reviews immediately after their first completed week.
4. Visit 5–10 local daycares/after-school programs in your service area in person, leave a card, ask about their front-desk recommendation policy.
5. Offer a "founding family" discount (e.g., locked-in lower recurring rate for life) to your first 10 signed recurring customers — creates urgency and reduces price sensitivity in early conversations.

## 50 customers
Layer in: small paid Facebook/Instagram geo-ads once you have 10+ reviews and testimonials; formal referral program launch; a second round of daycare/school-adjacent visits (sports leagues, tutoring centers) using your now-real customer testimonials as proof; ask happy customers if you can film a 30-second phone-video testimonial for social/website use.

## 100 customers
This is likely past what one vehicle can serve on recurring routes — treat hitting ~40–60 concurrent recurring students on one vehicle as your trigger to start the second-vehicle/driver hiring process (see Growth Plan) so growth capacity keeps pace with demand rather than you having to turn away or waitlist inbound leads. Continue Google/Facebook local SEO compounding, expand published service area to adjacent cities, consider a modest local sponsorship (a youth sports team jersey/banner sponsorship in your service area — visible, community-goodwill, inexpensive).

## 250 customers
This is a multi-vehicle, multi-driver stage (see Growth Plan for the 1→10 vehicle path). Marketing shifts toward brand/reputation maintenance (review management, community sponsorships, possible formal partnerships/contracts with schools or daycare chains) rather than direct-response acquisition tactics — by this point word-of-mouth and search ranking should be doing most of the acquisition work if the safety/reliability product held up.
