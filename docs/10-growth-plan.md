# Growth Plan — 1 Vehicle to Full Company

## 1 → 2 vehicles
Trigger: consistent waitlist/turned-away requests, or your one vehicle is booked past a sustainable daily schedule (recommend capping a single driver's daily route load well below legal driving-hour limits — you're not FMCSA-regulated at this size, but driver fatigue with children aboard is a real safety risk regardless). At this stage, hire your first driver. **Independent contractor vs. employee** is the key legal decision here: Florida (like the IRS/DOL generally) looks at behavioral and financial control, not just what you call the relationship. A driver who follows your set routes, your pricing, your safety protocols, drives your vehicle, and represents your brand looks like an **employee** under most tests, not a true independent contractor — misclassifying them as 1099 to avoid payroll tax/workers' comp is a common and costly mistake in exactly this kind of business (it's been litigated repeatedly against rideshare-adjacent companies). Budget for W-2 employment (payroll taxes, workers' comp once you cross FL's employee threshold, unemployment insurance) rather than assuming 1099 status will hold up. **VERIFY** classification with your accountant/attorney before hiring.

## 2 → 5 vehicles
Trigger: multiple recurring routes across more of your service area than one or two drivers can reasonably cover, and revenue supports vehicle financing/purchase without straining cash flow. Introduce: a simple dispatch process (even a shared calendar/spreadsheet at this size, graduating to the no-code platform's multi-driver assignment features), standardized driver onboarding (your safety-system checklist becomes a real onboarding packet, not tribal knowledge), and your first real bookkeeping/payroll system (QuickBooks or similar) if you haven't already.

## 5 → 10 vehicles
Trigger: service area expansion beyond what you personally can oversee day-to-day, or first B2B contract opportunities (a school district route, a daycare chain contract) that require fleet-level insurance certificates and possibly the Level 2/vendor-screening compliance work described in the legal doc. Introduce: a dedicated operations/dispatch role (no longer you personally coordinating every driver), a formal maintenance schedule across the fleet, and a real HR process (job descriptions, structured onboarding, performance review) since you're now managing people, not just driving.

## 10 vehicles → full transportation company
Trigger: you're running multiple service areas or a mixed fleet (larger vans, possibly CDL-threshold vehicles for group activity/camp routes), and considering B2B contracts as a real revenue line alongside direct-to-parent bookings. At this scale, revisit every item in the legal doc as if starting fresh — CDL requirements for larger vehicles, potential DOT-adjacent thresholds depending on vehicle mix, formal safety-management-system practices, and dedicated legal/insurance counsel on retainer rather than one-off consults. This is also the point to seriously evaluate the custom software build described in the Website Tech doc, since a no-code platform will likely be straining under real multi-city, multi-fleet dispatch complexity by now.

## Employee vs. contractor, summarized
- **Employees (W-2):** right model for drivers who follow your routes, schedule, pricing, vehicle, and brand — which is nearly every driver in this business model. Costs more upfront (payroll tax, workers' comp) but is the legally defensible structure and gives you real control over safety compliance (you can mandate training, background checks, and conduct standards as a condition of employment far more cleanly than as a contractor relationship).
- **Independent contractors (1099):** legally defensible only for someone who sets their own schedule, uses their own vehicle, and operates with real independence from your brand/pricing — which describes almost none of the driver relationships in a business built on consistent recurring child-safety standards. Avoid defaulting to this classification just because it's cheaper; get it reviewed.
