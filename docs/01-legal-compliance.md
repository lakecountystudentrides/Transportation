# Legal & Regulatory Research — Private Child Transportation, Lake County, FL

**Read this first:** This is research, not legal advice. Florida child-transportation law sits at the intersection of at least five separate regulatory regimes (traffic code, school-transportation code, child-care licensing, for-hire vehicle ordinances, and general business law), and none of them was written with a "book-a-ride app for kids" business in mind. Several of the questions below have no clean statewide answer — where that's true, this doc says so explicitly and names the office to call. **Before you accept your first paying customer, get 30–60 minutes with a Florida business attorney (ideally one who's worked with rideshare/NEMT/daycare-transport clients) and your insurance agent.** That spend (~$300–$600) is the single highest-leverage thing on this whole list.

Research date: September 2026. Laws change; re-verify anything marked VERIFY before you rely on it.

## 1–3. Vehicle classification — the most important legal fact in this whole document

Florida's "school bus" definition (F.S. **1006.25**) turns on **ownership/control by a district school board**, not on capacity or cargo. A privately owned van that you personally own and use to run a parent-paid shuttle service is **not** a school bus under Florida law, no matter how many kids ride in it (up to its designed seating capacity) or how often it goes to a school.

Separately, Florida's vehicle-equipment standards for pupil transportation (historically F.S. 234.05 / now folded into Ch. 1006, plus the FLDOE's annual *School Bus Specifications* document) apply to "motor vehicles with a seating capacity of less than 24 pupils that are regularly used for the transportation of pupils to or from school or school activities" — **but privately owned passenger motor vehicles are explicitly excluded from that requirement.** That's the exclusion that lets you operate a normal minivan/SUV/passenger van without retrofitting it to FLDOE school-bus spec (stop-arms, flashing lights, roof markings, etc.) — and you should **never** add those markings yourself; a vehicle equipped and marked like a school bus without being one creates confusion, liability, and could draw FLDOE/law-enforcement scrutiny.

There is a Florida Attorney General opinion directly on point, titled *"Private buses and vans transporting children"* (myfloridalegal.com/ag-opinions/private-buses-and-vans-transporting-children). I was not able to fetch its full text in this session (network-blocked from this environment) — **read it yourself before launch**; it's short and squarely on your fact pattern. Also pull the current-year FLDOE spec sheet (search "FLDOE School Bus Specifications 2026 PDF") to confirm the passenger-vehicle exclusion still reads the way described above.

**Bottom line:** minivan, SUV, or passenger van (≤15 seats including driver) = legally a private passenger vehicle for your purposes, not a school bus, and not subject to FLDOE bus-equipment retrofitting. Stay at or under that capacity and you avoid an entire regulatory regime.

**VERIFY:** Florida Department of Education, Office of Safety Schools/Student Transportation (fldoe.org) — ask specifically "does F.S. 1006.25 or any FLDOE rule reach a privately owned, non-district-contracted vehicle used to transport children to and from school for a fee paid by parents?" Get the answer in writing (email) and keep it in your compliance file.

## 4–11. Driver requirements, screening, training

**Legally required (state minimum, any driver, any vehicle):**
- Valid Florida driver's license, class matching the vehicle (see §23 CDL below).
- Compliance with FL traffic law generally (no suspensions, no open DUI, etc. — your insurer will check this via MVR regardless).

**Legally required only in specific contractual contexts:**
- **School district contracts:** F.S. **1012.465** requires Level 2 (fingerprint-based FDLE/FBI) background screening for any contracted, noninstructional personnel who have contact with students — this reaches bus/van contractors and their employees. If you ever sign on as a vendor to Lake County Schools or a charter school, this is non-negotiable and the district's Transportation/Risk Management office will walk you through their specific vendor-screening process. **VERIFY** current citation and process with Lake County Schools' Transportation Department directly — district vendor requirements are often stricter than the statutory floor.
- **Licensed child-care facilities (Ch. 402, Rule 65C-22):** if a daycare's *own* staff/vehicle does the driving, Ch. 65C-22 requires their drivers to have a valid license with proper endorsement, an annual physical, and current First Aid + Pediatric CPR certification, plus a transport log retained 12 months. This chapter regulates the **daycare's own** transportation program. It does **not** automatically require an independent third-party transporter (you) to be Level-2 screened by DCF — **but** any DCF-licensed facility you regularly pick up from/drop off at will have its own policies about who it releases a child to, and many centers will informally (or by their own internal policy) ask to see your background-check results before adding you to a child's authorized-pickup list. **VERIFY** with the specific Lake County DCF Child Care Regulation licensing counselor whether picking a child up *from* (not employed by) a licensed facility triggers any facility-side obligation you need to help them satisfy.

**Strongly recommended regardless of the above (do this for every driver, including yourself, before your first paid trip):**
- **Level 2-equivalent background check** through a private FDLE-authorized live-scan vendor (same fingerprint-based state + national criminal history + sex-offender registry check DCF uses). Cost is typically $50–$90 per driver. This is your #1 marketing asset and your #1 liability defense — treat it as required even though no single statute forces an independent operator to do it.
- **Motor vehicle record (MVR) check** at hire and at least annually after (most commercial auto insurers require this and will pull it themselves — ask your agent).
- **Drug and alcohol screening** pre-hire and random/post-incident, even though DOT/FMCSA testing rules don't apply to you at this vehicle size and for-hire class. Buy this from a local occupational-health clinic (~$40–$75/test).
- **First Aid + Pediatric/Adult CPR certification** (American Red Cross or American Heart Association, ~$35–$75, renew every 2 years).
- **Child passenger safety (CPS) technician training or, at minimum, a car-seat-installation course** — Safe Kids Worldwide runs free/low-cost classes in Central Florida; check safekids.org for a Lake/Orange County chapter event.
- A written driver code of conduct (no phone use while children aboard, no unauthorized stops, no smoking/vaping in vehicle, no unaccompanied errands, etc.) signed by every driver.

## 12–13. Vehicle inspection, seat belts, child restraints

- **Child restraint law (F.S. 316.613):** children under age 6 must ride in a federally approved, crash-tested child restraint (car seat or booster appropriate to size). Children age 6 and under 18 must be secured by a seat belt (F.S. 316.614). Florida does **not** have a statutory booster-seat mandate tied to height for the 6–8 age range the way many states do — but a seat belt that doesn't fit a small 6- or 7-year-old properly is a real safety and liability problem. **Operate to the stricter NHTSA/AAP guidance, not the statutory floor:** a child should stay in a booster until the vehicle seat belt fits correctly unassisted — typically around 4'9" and 8–12 years old. Put this in your written safety policy and enforce it regardless of what a parent requests.
- **Provide and install the restraint yourself** (don't rely on parents supplying/installing one) — buy multiple sizes (infant/rear-facing, convertible, high-back booster, backless booster) so you can match the child, and have every seat professionally inspected/installed per manufacturer instructions.
- **Vehicle inspection:** no general Florida state-mandated periodic inspection for a private passenger vehicle. Do your own documented pre-trip and monthly inspection anyway (tires, brakes, seat belts/anchors, child-lock function, first-aid kit, fire extinguisher) — insurers and school/daycare partners will ask if you have one. **VERIFY** whether Lake County's vehicle-for-hire permit (if one applies to you, see below) requires a periodic safety inspection as a condition of the permit.

## 14–17. Insurance

- **Your personal auto policy will not cover this business.** The moment you accept payment to transport someone else's child, you are operating a "livery" or "for-hire" use that is excluded under a standard personal auto policy. Driving even one paid trip on personal coverage risks a full claim denial after an accident.
- **Commercial auto liability policy, livery/for-hire/passenger-service rated**, is what you actually need. For-hire passenger vehicle policies in Florida commonly track around **$125,000 per person / $250,000 per incident bodily injury / $50,000 property damage** as a baseline used in several county for-hire ordinances — but that is a *floor*, not a target. For a business whose entire value proposition is child safety, carry materially more: **$1,000,000 combined single limit** commercial auto is a realistic minimum ask, and it's what most school/daycare vendor contracts require as a condition of doing business with them.
- **General liability (GL) insurance**, $1,000,000/$2,000,000 aggregate is a common small-business standard, covering non-auto incidents (a fall at pickup, etc.).
- **Sexual Abuse & Molestation (SAM) liability coverage** — ask for this **by name**. Many standard CGL policies explicitly *exclude* abuse/molestation claims unless you buy this endorsement separately. For a business alone with other people's children, this is not optional — treat its absence as a disqualifying gap in any quote you get.
- **Workers' compensation** — required under Florida law (F.S. 440) once you have 1+ non-owner employees in most industries (construction has a lower threshold; general business is 4+, but check current thresholds with the FL Division of Workers' Compensation as they periodically change, and note that even below the mandatory threshold most school/daycare contracts will still require you to carry it). If your drivers are independent contractors rather than employees, workers' comp doesn't legally apply the same way, but see the Growth Plan doc for why misclassifying drivers as 1099 contractors is a real legal risk for this kind of business.
- **Umbrella policy** ($1M+) once revenue and vehicle count grow — cheap layered protection on top of the above.

**VERIFY with a licensed Florida commercial insurance agent who has written policies for NEMT (non-emergency medical transport), daycare shuttle, or school-vendor transportation businesses specifically** — this is a narrow enough niche that a generalist agent may not know which carriers write it well in Central Florida. Ask specifically: (1) does the policy cover for-hire child transportation, (2) is SAM coverage included or endorsed, (3) what's excluded for car-seat use/installation.

## 18–22. Business licensing, Lake County, cities, and the vehicle-for-hire question

- **Florida LLC:** done (per your message).
- **Lake County Local Business Tax Receipt (BTR)**, formerly called an "occupational license" — required for any business operating in Lake County. Apply through the **Lake County Tax Collector's office (laketax.com)**. Cost is modest (typically well under $100/year) and classification matters — ask specifically for the transportation-services classification code so you're not misfiled under general retail/service.
- **Municipal BTR:** if your business address (home office, garage where the van is kept, etc.) sits inside an incorporated city rather than unincorporated Lake County — Clermont, Leesburg, Eustis, Mount Dora, Tavares, Groveland, Minneola, Fruitland Park, Astatula, or Umatilla — that city will *also* require its own BTR in addition to the county one. **VERIFY** your exact address's jurisdiction (county vs. city) and apply to both if applicable.
- **Vehicle-for-hire permit — the open question.** Florida counties are authorized by general law to license/regulate taxis, jitneys, limousines, and other for-hire passenger vehicles operating in their unincorporated areas (this is how Palm Beach, Hillsborough, and Miami-Dade Counties each run their own vehicle-for-hire programs). **I could not confirm, from public search, whether Lake County currently has its own vehicle-for-hire ordinance, and if so what it requires** (permit fee, vehicle inspection, driver background check, insurance filing, etc.). Note also that **F.S. Chapter 2024-28 (HB 377, effective July 1, 2024)** now lets someone who already holds a for-hire vehicle license/permit from *any* Florida county or municipality operate in other FL jurisdictions without a second permit — so if a neighboring county's process is easier to find, that reciprocity may matter to you.
  **ACTION ITEM before you take your first booking:** call **Lake County's Office of Business Licensing / County Manager's Office** (and, separately, your city's clerk if you're inside a municipality) and ask point-blank: *"I'm running a private, parent-paid, non-school-district passenger transportation service for children, one vehicle, from [address]. Do I need a vehicle-for-hire permit, a chauffeur's license, or any transportation-specific permit beyond a standard Business Tax Receipt?"* Get the answer in writing. This is the one item in this whole document I'd flag as "do not skip, do not assume."
- **Transportation Network Company (TNC) law (F.S. 627.748)** governs services like Uber/Lyft and preempts local regulation of *those* — it almost certainly does not describe your model (you're not dispatching independent drivers through an app marketplace; you're a direct carrier), so don't rely on TNC registration as your compliance path. Mentioned here only so you don't confuse the two regimes when researching.

## 23–24. CDL and passenger limits

- Florida (aligned with federal FMCSA definitions) requires a **Commercial Driver's License** for a vehicle **designed to transport 16 or more passengers, including the driver**, regardless of whether it's for-hire. Below that threshold — a standard minivan, SUV, or passenger van seating 15 or fewer including the driver — **no CDL is required**, even though you're being paid.
- **Practical recommendation:** stay well under that line. A minivan or full-size van in the 6–11 passenger range keeps you simply, comfortably licensed with a standard Class E Florida driver's license, avoids CDL medical-certificate and hours-of-service questions entirely, and matches your actual near-term route sizes (you're picking up individual kids or small siblings groups, not running a 14-seat shuttle).
- **VERIFY** the current CDL passenger threshold and any Florida-specific carve-outs directly with **FLHSMV's Commercial Driver License office** (flhsmv.gov) before purchasing any vehicle seating 12+ — thresholds and GVWR interactions have moved before and it's a cheap phone call against an expensive mistake.

## 25–29. Contracts, pickup/dropoff, authorization, and campus access

- No state law requires a written contract for a private parent-to-provider transportation deal — but you should **never** run a trip without one. See the Booking/Website doc for exactly what your Parent Service Agreement + per-child authorization form needs to say (this is a business-design decision, not a licensing one).
- **Home pickup/dropoff:** legal, no special permit needed. Practical safety design (verified adult present, PIN/code confirmation) is covered in the Safety System section of the Business Plan doc.
- **School/daycare campus dropoff:** every campus sets its own visitor and vehicle-line rules. Before your first pickup at a *given* school or daycare, contact their front office, explain you're an independent, parent-hired transporter (not a district bus), and ask what their procedure is for releasing a child to a non-parent, non-bus vehicle. Many schools require you to be listed as an "authorized pickup" on that specific child's file, sometimes with photo ID shown at the office rather than curbside. This is a per-campus administrative step, not a county/state license.
- **Written parental authorization:** not state-mandated for you, but functionally your most important legal document — see the booking-system doc for the fields to collect (authorized pickup/dropoff adults, standing instructions, medical info limited to what's transportation-relevant, e.g., allergies/seizure protocol/mobility needs — not a general medical history).

## 30. Data privacy and recordkeeping

- **Florida Information Protection Act (F.S. 501.171):** once you store personal information electronically (names, addresses, payment data, etc. — which you will, the moment you have a website with a database), you're legally required to maintain reasonable security measures and to notify affected individuals (and, above a threshold, the Florida Attorney General) within 30 days of a confirmed data breach. Build this into your vendor selection (see Website Tech doc) — using an established, PCI-compliant platform for payments (Stripe/Square) meaningfully reduces your own breach exposure versus rolling your own payment storage.
- **COPPA (federal):** governs online services that collect data *directly from* children under 13. Because parents/guardians are the ones creating accounts and entering their child's information on your site — not the children themselves — COPPA's operator obligations (parental consent flows, etc.) likely don't attach in the way they would for, say, a kids' game app. **Do not** design any feature that has the *child* interact with your website/app directly (no child login, no child-facing chat) — that would pull COPPA in. **VERIFY** this read with your attorney once your feature set (e.g., any child-facing app component) is finalized.
- **Recordkeeping:** keep signed consent/authorization forms, background-check results, MVR checks, training certificates, and trip logs for the life of the driver relationship plus several years after (ask your insurer and attorney for the exact retention period they want given Florida's minor-tolling rules on the statute of limitations — this can extend well past a normal business record-retention policy).

## Summary: REQUIRED vs. RECOMMENDED vs. DO NOT

**Legally REQUIRED, no exceptions:**
- Florida LLC (have it) + Lake County BTR + city BTR if applicable
- Commercial (not personal) auto insurance for the vehicle
- Child restraints per F.S. 316.613/316.614
- Standard FL driver's license appropriate to vehicle size (no CDL under 16 total occupants)
- Workers' comp once you cross FL's employee-count threshold (if hiring W-2 drivers)
- Reasonable data security + breach notification under F.S. 501.171 once you store customer data electronically

**Legally required only if/when you contract with a school district or licensed child-care facility as their vendor:**
- Level 2 fingerprint background screening (F.S. 1012.465, district vendor rules)
- Whatever facility-specific driver credentials that district/daycare's contract demands

**Unresolved — verify before launch:**
- Whether Lake County (or your specific city) requires a vehicle-for-hire permit/chauffeur license beyond a standard BTR
- Exact language of the current FLDOE School Bus Specifications passenger-vehicle exclusion, and the full text of the myfloridalegal.com AG opinion on private buses/vans

**Strongly RECOMMENDED (treat as required for a credible, insurable child-transport business):**
- Level 2-equivalent background check + MVR + drug screening for every driver, including yourself, from day one
- CPR/First Aid certification for every driver
- $1M+ commercial auto + $1M+ GL with Sexual Abuse & Molestation coverage named explicitly
- Written parent service agreement + per-child authorization form + pickup/dropoff verification protocol
- Documented vehicle pre-trip inspection routine

**DO NOT, without additional licensing/approval:**
- Do not equip or mark your vehicle to look like a school bus (stop-arm, flashing lights, "SCHOOL BUS" signage) — you are not one, and doing so invites both legal exposure and confusion with actual district buses.
- Do not exceed your vehicle's manufacturer-rated seating capacity under any circumstance, regardless of children's small size.
- Do not operate on a personal auto policy once you accept your first paid booking.
- Do not represent to parents, schools, or daycares that you are "licensed by the State of Florida" for pupil transportation unless/until you've confirmed with FLDOE/Lake County exactly what license, if any, actually applies to your model — overstating your regulatory status is itself a legal risk.
- Do not add a child-facing login/interaction to the website/app without re-clearing it against COPPA with your attorney.

## Who to actually call, in order

1. **Lake County Office of Business Licensing / County Manager's Office** — vehicle-for-hire permit question (see §18–22).
2. **Your specific city's Clerk/Finance office** (only if your business address is inside a municipality) — local BTR.
3. **A Florida business/transportation attorney** — 30–60 minute paid consult to confirm the AG opinion's application to your exact model, your parent contract language, and your COPPA read.
4. **A Florida-licensed commercial insurance agent with NEMT/daycare-shuttle experience** — commercial auto + GL + SAM coverage quote.
5. **Lake County Schools District, Transportation/Risk Management Department** — only once you're pursuing an actual district contract; ask their vendor-screening requirements directly (F.S. 1012.465 process).
6. **DCF Child Care Regulation, regional licensing counselor covering Lake County** — only if you plan to be added to authorized-pickup lists at specific licensed daycare centers, to ask whether they need anything from you.
7. **FLHSMV Commercial Driver License office** — only if/when you consider any vehicle seating 12+ passengers.
